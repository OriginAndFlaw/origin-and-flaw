Why does a bridge built fifty years ago already need repairs,
when Roman concrete still stands after two thousand years?
The answer is something we added on purpose. Steel.
Steel bars let us build thin bridges, tall towers and long highways.
But when water and salt reach that steel, it rusts.
Rust takes up more space than steel.
So it pushes the concrete apart from the inside.
The Romans had no steel bars. Nothing inside to rust.
Now scientists are studying their recipe to make concrete that lasts.
