Modern concrete hates seawater.
Salt creeps in, reaches the steel inside, and the steel starts to rust.
Rust takes up more space than steel, so it cracks the concrete from the inside.
Many sea walls built in the last century are already falling apart.
But Roman harbour walls have stood in the sea for two thousand years.
Seawater has been seeping through them all that time.
And instead of destroying the concrete, it helped it.
The water reacted with volcanic ash in the mix and grew new crystals inside the cracks.
The sea made it stronger.
