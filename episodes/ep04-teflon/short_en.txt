The pan in your kitchen helped build the atomic bomb.
In 1938, a chemist opened a cylinder of gas. Nothing came out. But it was still heavy.
He sawed it open and found a white, waxy powder that nothing would stick to.
Nobody wanted it. Until the Manhattan Project needed a material that uranium hexafluoride, one of the most corrosive gases ever handled, could not destroy.
That powder sealed the valves of the bomb factory at Oak Ridge.
After the war, it got a name. Teflon.
