One phone call. A few seconds. A hundred and fourteen lives.
A steel company building a hotel in Kansas City asked for a small change.
Instead of one long rod holding two walkways, use two short rods.
According to the fabricator, the engineers said yes. Over the phone.
Nobody sat down and did the maths.
That small change doubled the load on a single nut.
Two years later, on a crowded Friday night, the walkways fell.
The engineers lost their licences.
And today, every engineering student learns this rule: a change is a new design. Check it like one.
